The fundamental DBP architecture was established with the Alternating Direction
Method of Multipliers (ADMM) and Conjugate Gradient (CG) solvers~\cite{decentralized2017}. A central unit sums the local results and returns them to the clusters. ADMM uses an explicit local inversion, and CG avoids it~\cite{decentralized2017}.
% FONTE: decentralized2017 (li2017_jetcas) L239 e L329 (consenso na unidade central); L421 e L485 ("ADMM methods also involve an explicit matrix inversion step").
The Partial and Full Decentralization (PD and FD) architectures were formalized
in~\cite{jeon2019}. In PD, a central unit sums the local vectors $\mathbf H_c^H\mathbf y_c$ and Gram matrices $\mathbf H_c^H\mathbf H_c$ (Section~\ref{subsec:equalization}) and equalizes. In FD, each cluster equalizes locally, and the central unit takes a weighted average of the estimates \cite{jeon2019}.
% FONTE: jeon2019 L53, L105 (y_c^MRC = H_c^H y_c e G_c = H_c^H H_c) e L119.

Other works use the chain topology, in which the estimate passes from one cluster to the next. A recursive MMSE chain
uses an $M_c \times M_c$ inversion per cluster, achieves
centralized-MMSE equivalence, and supports early termination based on channel quality~\cite{zheng2022}. The Groupwise Recursive Least Squares (GRLS) algorithm extends RLS from one to several antennas per DU~\cite{chen2024grls}.
% FONTE: zheng2022 L8 (resumo); chen2024grls L8 (resumo).

Some algorithms avoid the inversion.
In the daisy-chain coordinate-descent algorithm, each node updates the estimate with its own data, without a central node~\cite{rodriguezsanchez2020}. The Kaczmarz method solves $\mathbf y = \mathbf H\mathbf s$ one equation at a time: each node corrects the estimate with its own equation and passes it on. The Bayesian Distributed Kaczmarz algorithm also estimates and removes the noise at each iteration \cite{croisfelt2021}.
% FONTE: croisfelt2021 L8 (resumo, receptor bayesiano) e L78-L79 (cada nó conhece a m-ésima equação; atualização de Kaczmarz).
The Groupwise Expectation Propagation (GW-EP) algorithm divides the users into groups and detects each group separately, in star and daisy-chain topologies~\cite{li2023iotj}.
\textcolor{GREEN}{A more recent work addresses} spatial correlation and imperfect CSI with a star topology, in which a central unit fuses the outputs of the \textcolor{GREEN}{cluster LMMSE receivers}~\cite{zhuang2025}.
% FONTE: rodriguezsanchez2020 L8 (sem nó central, recursivo); li2023iotj L8 (grupos, fusão na CPU); zhuang2025 L8 (resumos).

All the articles cited above assume a diagonal noise covariance matrix.
To the best of the author's knowledge, only two works simultaneously address colored noise and
decentralized LMMSE equalization~\cite{zhao2021-globecom,zhao2025jsac}. The BCD-MMSE algorithm was introduced for the daisy-chain architecture~\cite{zhao2021-globecom}. It requires a local $M_c \times M_c$ Hermitian matrix inversion
at each DU. \textcolor{GREEN}{A later work extends the framework} with the sDR-MMSE and cDR-MMSE algorithms. In the star topology, these algorithms reduce the data each DU sends from the number of antennas to the number of users \cite{zhao2025jsac}. That work shows that BCD-MMSE comes close to the SER of centralized LMMSE.
